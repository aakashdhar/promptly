Write the summary under exactly these seven headings, in this order, each on its own line:
## The project
## People
## How they like to be written to
## Agreed
## Open right now
## Latest activity
## Words

What goes in each:
- The project: what it is, for whom, the current phase, milestones and key dates.
- People: everyone involved on both sides, client side first. Start each line with the person's name, then organisation and role in brackets, then anything else: "<name> (<organisation>, <role>) — <email address, what they own or decide>".
- How they like to be written to: how the client and other key people write and what they expect back: formal or casual, greeting and sign-off, length, language, who to copy, how quickly they expect replies. Only what their own messages show.
- Agreed: scope, decisions, prices, rates, payment terms and deadlines both sides accepted.
- Open right now: unanswered questions, pending approvals, promised work not yet delivered, and who each one is waiting on.
- Latest activity: only what happened from {FROM} to {TODAY}, newest first, each line starting with its date. Nothing older. If nothing in the material falls in that window, leave the section empty.
- Words: names of products, systems, teams, acronyms and terms, spelled exactly. Start each line with the term, then what it is: "<term> — <what it is>".

Rules:
- Use only the material. Never invent, guess or generalise beyond it. If the material doesn't say something, leave it out; don't write "unknown" or "not mentioned".
- Copy names, dates, amounts and spellings exactly. Write dates as calendar dates ("19 Nov"), never "last week" or "recently".
- When facts disagree, the newer source wins. Mention the earlier value only when the change matters, e.g. "moved from 12 Nov to 19 Nov".
- Merge lines that say the same thing. Don't repeat a fact in two sections; Latest activity may still say what happened to an item listed elsewhere.
- Each line is a "- " bullet holding one fact or a tight group of related facts, at most 40 words.
- Every bullet ends with the source tags of the facts it rests on, copied exactly from the material: [source: <path> · <date>]. Cite only files named in the material. When a bullet rests on more than two files, use one tag per top folder instead, counting the files: [source: <folder> · <number> emails], or files when they aren't all emails. Files at the top of the project (no folder) always keep their own tags.
- At most {WORDS} words in all, not counting source tags. In each section put first what matters most for writing to these people now; drop minor detail before anything important.
- A section with nothing to say keeps its heading with no lines under it.

Output only the summary in markdown, starting with "## The project": no title, preamble, notes or code fences.
